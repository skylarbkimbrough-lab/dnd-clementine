Who She Is

Clem is a half-drow druid operating somewhere in the grey space between lawful society and the deep, indifferent wild. She was raised in a druid tribe of the frozen places — a grove that understood Silvanus not as warmth and green things but as permafrost, killing cold, and the kind of silence that only exists miles from anyone who could help you. She carries that with her.

She owns and runs a bar with a small apothecary tucked behind it — a place where the remedies and the poisons share a shelf, separated only by her mood. She answers to no one. This is not negotiable.

She is chaotic in the truest sense: not destructive for destruction's sake, but genuinely, philosophically allergic to being told what to do. She has made a life out of existing in spaces that belong to her — her bar, her forest, her carefully maintained neutrality — and she intends to keep it that way.

Appearance

Chalk-white drow skin, blonde hair swept half-up and secured with an orange scrunchie that has no business looking that good on someone who carries two swords. Her eyes are deep crimson — a drow inheritance she has made no effort to hide or explain. A scatter of freckles crosses her nose and cheeks, warm against the pallor. Layered pearl necklaces sit at her throat. A dark coat. Two blades crossed at her back.

She looks like someone who owns the room. She does — she built it.

The Drow Question

Half-drow is a complicated inheritance. The Underdark carries baggage that follows you to the surface: the sidelong looks, the assumptions, the reflexive distrust from people who have heard the stories. Clem has developed what might generously be called a defense mechanism — she fills every room before anyone can define the atmosphere for her. She is louder than the whispers. She is warmer than the reputation.

Her mother was drow nobility — powerful, matriarchal, and dangerous in the way that only people with real authority can be. Her father, a surface-dweller who had loved the wrong woman, got them out. He brought Clem up into the light before her mother's world could fully claim her. There is a scar beneath his eye, and when she looks at it, she remembers exactly why they left.

She misses parts of it anyway. Grief doesn't require that the thing you lost was good. She knows this. She keeps moving.

Magic cannot put her to sleep, and enchantments that would bend another's will simply slide off her. She sees 120 feet in the dark. She has sunlight sensitivity, which she manages. She doesn't talk about the Underdark.

Patron Deity · Silvanus
Silvanus
The Oak Father · God of the Frozen Wild · Neutral

Silvanus is the god of wild nature in all its forms — including the kind that kills you slowly in a blizzard and doesn't apologize. He doesn't favor the warm and verdant over the frozen and merciless. The wild is the wild. Clem's grove understood this.

She keeps a shard of ice — preserved somehow, always cold to the touch — somewhere on her person at all times. She doesn't talk about where she got it. She doesn't talk about the grove much at all.

Traits & Nature
The Freedom Principle

Answers to no one. Built a bar so she'd always have somewhere to go that's hers. Built an apothecary so she'd never need to ask for anything.

Druid's Communion

Speaks to animals. The forest tells her things. She meditates for 4 hours instead of sleeping and wakes knowing things she shouldn't.

Social Animal

Flirts with everyone. Means it with no one in particular. It's not manipulation — it's just how she moves through the world.

The Chaos Factor

The party tolerates her. The party loves her. The party has learned to brace for impact whenever she smiles like that.

The Establishment

Clem's bar is hers in the way that matters — built, stocked, and run on her own terms. The apothecary in the back does a brisk business. She doesn't ask too many questions about what people need the tinctures for. The bar up front is warm, the drinks are strong, and the atmosphere is whatever she decides it is that evening.

Security is handled.

Bad Bunny

Bouncer. Loyal. Does not elaborate. Has never once needed to.
